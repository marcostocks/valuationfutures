Technical & Due-Diligence Overview

How Marco Valuation Futures works

Marco is a dated futures venue on private, pre-IPO valuations. Traders take leveraged long/short exposure on a company's total enterprise valuation; at the real listing or acquisition, every position cash-settles against a verifiable outcome. This page explains the architecture, the pricing and risk engine, and the on-chain program — and maps each claim to the exact place in the source so it can be independently verified.

02 · Anatomy of a trade

From deposit to settlement, and every risk check in between.
  1. Deposit margin
    The trader deposits USDC into a position account (created on first deposit). Margin is isolated — it backs only this position. deposit_collateral
  2. Open a position
    The trader picks a side and leverage. The notional is swapped against the vAMM (constant-product), which sets the average fill price and moves the mark. A taker fee is charged and routed to the insurance fund; the open is rejected if it exceeds the leverage cap or the per-side open-interest cap. open_position
  3. Marked continuously
    The position is valued against the mark price every tick. Unrealized PnL = current notional − cost basis (long) or the inverse (short). This drives the on-screen PnL and the liquidation check.
  4. Liquidation (if it goes wrong)
    If account equity (margin + unrealized PnL) falls below the maintenance margin, anyone may liquidate the position permissionlessly. The liquidator earns a reward; residual equity returns to the owner; the insurance fund covers any deficit. liquidate_position
  5. Close, or settle at the event
    The trader may close any time (realizing PnL against the vAMM). Otherwise, at the real IPO/acquisition the market is settled at a posted price and every position pays out at that fixed number. close_position · settle_market · settle_position

03 · The mark price

The reference used for PnL, margin and liquidation. Private equity has no live spot feed, so the mark is built from two sources.

The mark is internal by default, external by override:

  • Internal — an 8-hour EMA of the order book (a volume-weighted median of executed trades, smoothed). It is the market's own, permissionless, real-time estimate of the valuation. Anti-manipulation filters (unique-trader ratio, minimum order size, outlier rejection) and a thin-book freeze keep a spike or a hollow book from moving it.
  • External — the last verified hard transaction: a priced funding round, a qualifying secondary block, or a corporate action. It moves only on a confirmed event, and when it does the mark converges to the new anchor over a window (not a jump) to avoid liquidation cascades.

Why it fits a dated future

A perpetual has no expiry, so it needs a funding rate and a continuous external blend to stay tethered to reality. A dated future is tethered by convergence at settlement — the payoff is fixed to a verifiable value at expiry, so price must converge there regardless of interim book noise. That lets the mark stay a fast internal signal between events, with the external anchor reserved for genuine repricings and for settlement itself.

Implementation. The demo implements this end-to-end — a live indicator under the price shows which source is driving the mark (Internal · External override · Frozen · Settled), and event buttons let a reviewer trigger a funding round or a thin book. Demo timescales are compressed (seconds, not hours) so the behavior is visible in a sitting. Execution still fills against the vAMM (takers pay price impact), while the mark is the EMA — which is why a large order no longer skews the displayed valuation. The full spec is in the demo's Docs tab.

04 · Risk, margin & solvency

How the venue stays solvent when it is the counterparty to every trade.

Margin & liquidation

  • Isolated margin. Each position has its own collateral; a loss on one can never propagate to another. A trader holds at most one position per market — increasing adds to it, and flipping sides requires a full close first.
  • Initial margin = 1 / max leverage. Up to 10× (≥10% margin), fixed per market at creation.
  • Maintenance margin is set in basis points of notional. When equity falls below it, the position is liquidatable.
  • Permissionless liquidation. Any keeper can close an underwater position and earn a reward — no privileged actor required.

The two invariants

1 · Solvency by construction. No instruction ever transfers more USDC out of a vault than it holds. Payouts are clamped to the available balance and any gap is an explicit, emitted shortfall — never an overdraft. This is enforced directly in the code, e.g. in liquidation:

let insurance_cover = bad_debt.min(insurance_bal);
let mut available   = vault_bal.saturating_add(insurance_cover);
let reward_paid     = reward.min(available);
let trader_refund   = trader_refund_owed.min(available);

Every payout is a .min(balance) — the program cannot be made to pay out money it does not have.

2 · Per-market isolation. Margin, open interest, vAMM reserves and the insurance fund are all per-market and held in separate program-owned vaults. A blow-up in one market is ring-fenced and cannot touch another.

Backstops

  • Insurance fund — a per-market USDC vault, seeded permissionlessly and fed by all trading fees, that absorbs the residual skew the vAMM leaves open.
  • Governance — the admin authority is expected to be a Squads multisig. It can create markets, halt trading, settle, and rotate the keeper; it cannot pay funds to itself. The keeper (operator) can settle and liquidate but likewise cannot move funds to itself.
  • Kill-switches — a protocol-wide pause and a per-market halt block new exposure while still allowing closes, liquidations and settlement.

05 · Settlement & fallback logic

A dated future: it resolves to a single verifiable outcome, with explicit rules for the non-standard ones.

At the real event, the admin/operator posts the final valuation and the market moves to Settled; each position then settles at that fixed price — longs profit above their entry, shorts below. The displayed countdown is the expected listing date and is indicative only; settlement is contingent on the realised event.

OutcomeSettlement basis
IPO — standardRebase to per-share at the prospectus; settle against the first-day public close.
IPO delayed > 24 monthsConvert to NAV settlement via three independent valuation firms.
M&A / acquisitionSettle at the acquisition enterprise value (publicly disclosed).
Direct listingUse the reference price (not the opening trade).
IPO withdrawnContract voided; initial margin returned with no PnL.

Security posture

  • Checked arithmetic throughout. All ratio and PnL math runs in u128/i128 with checked_* / saturating_* operations, narrowing to u64 only at the edges with bounds checks. overflow-checks = true in the release profile.
  • Payouts clamped to balance — the solvency invariant from §4, enforced at every fund-moving instruction.
  • Authority separation — admin (multisig) governs; the keeper can liquidate/settle; neither can pay funds to itself.
  • Forward-compatible layout — every account carries explicit byte-size math and reserved padding for upgrades.
  • Auditable events — holder-initiated actions emit events (deposits, opens, closes, liquidations, settlements) for off-chain indexing.
  • Static analysis — fund-moving instructions were run through Solana program static analysis during development.

What the tests cover

  • Protocol config initializes; a market is created with mark == anchor valuation.
  • A long opens, counter-flow lifts the mark, and the long closes in profit.
  • A position above the leverage cap is rejected.
  • An underwater long is liquidated when the mark crashes.
  • A market settles — long wins, short loses, and the pool stays solvent.

One honest delta. The internal/external EMA marking described in §3 is implemented in the demo and the design; the on-chain program currently marks positions off the vAMM. Bringing the EMA + external-override marking on-chain (via order-book aggregation or a signed anchor) is the next protocol milestone. The margin, liquidation, custody, insurance and settlement machinery is identical either way.

Net: the economic core is real, tested, and legible; the pricing engine is fully specified and demonstrable; and the remaining work — chain wiring, on-chain marking, audit, deployment — is scoped and sequenced.
